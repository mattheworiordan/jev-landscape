## summary
(Not used: analyze.py generates the headline numbers from the data, so a refresh updates them.)

## changes
These are the Sonnet v1 to v2 changes, the rubric change the review asked for; Opus labelled with the v2 rubric. measured_production fell from 1.6% to 0.2% of cards, and production claims from 4.7% to 1.8%. Evidence and baseline agree more with the 120 hand labels (evidence 84.2% to 94.2%, baseline 81.7% to 89.2%), and v2 over-grades "measured" much less (3 of 35 measured cards on the sample, against 13 of 44 in v1). Tier is unchanged at 67.5%. realtime_infra moved the other way from what the rubric intended: Sonnet v2 flags 29.8% of all cards against v1's 25.8%, because it flags almost every game and more trading bots. The framing shifts are not reported: framing is withdrawn (section 4).

## base
Opus calls 347 posts not_a_jev_build, against 234 for Sonnet v2: local clones, distillations, "Jev-compatible" APIs over other models, and builds on Laya, Gemini, Qwen or DeepSeek. 209 of them Sonnet also calls not_a_jev_build. Most were other_or_meta in v1 (199) or games (50). The most viewed is a distilled 4B "Jev-style" local model (426,247 views), followed by a local Jev clone (206,466 views). On the audit's labels, Opus's not_a_jev_build label holds about two times in three (review/audit-opus.md, sanity check 3). Duplicates are few under the specified rule (7).

## families
Four families hold 65% of posts: other_or_meta (22.4%), games (19.1%), classification, routing and triage (14.0%) and search, rerank and extraction (9.3%). Views and likes do not follow posts, and the two measures agree. Browser and computer use has 4.5% of posts, 13.2% of views and 13.2% of likes. Voice, collaboration and compaction draw 2.3 to 2.8 times their post share of views and 2.4 to 2.7 times of likes, and trading 1.6 to 1.8 times. Games and other_or_meta draw about two thirds of their share. Family is the least stable label after tier: Opus agrees with the audit on 82% of the audited cards (Sonnet v2 71%), and on 79% of the voice, live chat and collaboration cards (Sonnet v2 58%).

## evidence
65.6% of posts show a build with no numbers and 30.8% report a number from the author's own demo or experiment. Measured demos draw 49.3% of views and 44.8% of likes. Opus labels 37 posts measured production, but the audit agrees with only 11 of the 20 of them it read, so the count this report uses is the audit's: 8 posts, 0.14%, at most 2.0%. Evidence is the most stable field after production_claim: Opus agrees with the audit on 93% of the audited cards.

## evidence_by_family
Data and telemetry (65% measured), trading (46%), classification (40%) and compaction (40%) are the most measured families. Collaboration (17%), live chat (18%) and other_or_meta (18%) are the least.

## baseline
On Opus's labels 80.4% of posts compare Jev with nothing; the audit's estimate is 80.0% (77.4 to 81.6%). Of the 1,097 posts that make a comparison, 630 (57%) compare with a frontier LLM, the weakest baseline for a narrow decision. Rules or regex (138), classic ML (59) and vendor APIs (31) together are 228 posts (4.1%), and the audit's estimate is 3.7% (3.0 to 5.4%). All four of Opus's shares are inside the audit's intervals.

## baseline_by_family
Agent harness (24%), browser (22%), classification (19%) and compaction (17%) compare with a frontier LLM most often. Search (7%) names rules most often. Classic ML and vendor APIs are 3% or less in every family.

## concentration
Views are highly concentrated (Gini 0.94) and so are likes (0.93). 47 cards hold half of all views, the median card has 133 views and 2 likes, and 44.9% of cards have under 100 views. The top-1% share is robust: it stays between 47.0% and 53.3% whether we measure by likes, drop the top card, drop the suspect cards, or use all 5,950 raw cards, and the audit's recount on its own base gives 53.4%.

## by_day
Post age drives the counts. The median post from 16 September has 793 views and from 22 September 93. Posts from 16 to 19 September are half of all posts and hold 79% of views and 78% of likes. Late-week families look less popular than they are. Trading is the clearest case: its daily count peaked on 21 and 22 September (54 and 50 posts, against 28 to 37 a day from 17 to 20 September).

## top20
9 of the 20 most-viewed cards are measured demos, 10 are demos with no numbers and 1 is commentary; none is measured production. Browser and computer use (5), classification (3) and other_or_meta (3) are the most common families.

## realtime
Games are 67% of the cards Opus flags realtime. Outside games, trading (104) and voice (69) are the largest groups. Opus labels 2 realtime cards outside games measured production: the audit re-read one, a live news feed, and it does not hold; it never saw the other, a launch-alert model for trading. None of the 8 production posts that hold on the audit is a realtime build. On the first review's re-read of v1, 3 of v1's 16 realtime production cards held up; 9 of the 16 were trading bots that trade the author's own money.

## realtime_families
On Opus's labels the three realtime families are 3.7% of posts (Sonnet v2: 2.7%). The audit's census and negative sample put them at 4.0% (2.9 to 6.6%), with none measured in production; on the audit's own labels two claim production without a measurement, a live news feed and a Discord moderation bot. Japanese posts are 32% to 36% of these families against 19.6% overall. They draw more attention than their size (7.9% of views).

## production
Three readings set the range. The audit's re-read keeps 8 of the 15 strict Sonnet v2 cards (0.14% of posts), and its samples allow at most 2.0%. Opus labels all 8 production, and 29 more that the audit does not confirm: of Opus's 37, the audit read 20 and agrees with 11, 3 of those on first-pass labels its estimate does not count. The first review's re-read of v1's 95 production cards kept 51 (0.9% of v1's 5,782 cards), a looser reading that the audit did not repeat, and the first pass's 1.6% is the ceiling of the looser labels. The 8 that hold are a task router, a deploy approval gate, a news pipeline, a search reranker, an episode recommender, a fleet-wide decision layer and two public Q&A sites. None is a realtime build.

## posts_per_day
Posting peaked on 19 September (1,027 posts in the base) and fell to 628 on 22 September. 16 September has only 89 posts: the feed's first post is at 00:28 UTC, about 6 hours after launch, and posting ramped up from 17 September. 23 September is a partial day (last post 18:44 UTC).

## language
English is 68.7% of posts, Japanese 19.6% and Chinese 6.5%. Chinese posts draw 12.2% of views but 8.2% of likes, and English posts draw 74.7% of likes from 68.7% of posts. Japanese is highest in voice and collaboration (36% each) and live chat (32%). Chinese is highest in trading (14%), live chat (12%) and browser (11%).

## chips
The two multiples are the only chip medians we keep, and they agree with OpenChamber's survey within a factor of about 1.2. The "1×" chips are an extractor artefact (51 of the 297 speed chips and 8 of the 126 cost chips on this base). Dropping them moves the medians to 34× and 8.1×. The audit recounted both medians on its own base (28× and 6×), and they hold.

## agreement
Jev picks Opus's family for 78.4% of cards (kappa 0.75), against 76.7% for Sonnet v2. Games (F1 0.90), trading (0.90) and browser (0.82) match best, and live chat (0.45), voice (0.54) and data and telemetry (0.64) worst. Jev folds data cards into classification and many meta cards into games or classification. The largest disagreements are cards Opus calls other_or_meta that Jev calls games (95) or classification (87).

## calibration
Agreement rises steadily with Jev's stated probability, from 42.5% in the lowest decile to 98.0% in the highest. The two highest deciles (probability 0.99 or more) agree 97.1% and 98.0% of the time, but 38% of the cards at 0.99 or more are games or trading, the easiest families. Against the 120 hand labels, Jev is right on 27 of 28 at 0.99 or more; on the audit's 464 cards at 0.99 or more, it matches the audit's family on 455 (98.1%). This measures consistency with a reader, not accuracy against a human.

## hand_check
Opus agrees with the 120 hand labels more than any Sonnet run on every field (family 92.5% against 73.3% for Sonnet v2; tier 78.3% against 67.5%). That gap overstates it: the hand labels were written by Claude Opus 5.5 in a separate review run, so they share Opus's reading. The audit (1,681 cards, Grok) is the independent check, and there the gap is smaller but in the same direction (family 82% against 71%). Sonnet v2 is not deterministic: between pilot 2 and the full run, on the same 120 cards and the same rubric, family matched on 102 and tier on 98, so a gap of about 5 points between two runs is noise. The author's own labels (review/human-labels.jsonl, 13 cards so far) are a calibration, not a sample; they are what withdrew framing and the seven-level tier.

## ladder
Two thirds of posts (68.5%) show no measurement on Opus's labels, and they hold 49.7% of views; measured demos are 30.8% of posts and hold 49.3% of the views. The audit puts no measurement at 67.3% (63.5 to 70.5%) and measured demos at 32.5% (29.4 to 36.3%). It splits the unmeasured posts that are not meta into demos with no numbers (46.6% of all posts) and cost, speed or accuracy claims with no number (2.5%). The top rung is nearly empty: 8 posts on the audit's re-read.
