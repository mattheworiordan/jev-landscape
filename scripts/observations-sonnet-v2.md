## summary
(Not used: analyze.py generates the headline numbers from the data, so a refresh updates them.)

## changes
The new rubric moved measured production and production claims a lot. measured_production fell from 1.6% to 0.2%, and production claims from 4.7% to 1.8%. Evidence and baseline agree more with the hand labels (evidence 84.2% to 94.2%, baseline 81.7% to 89.2%), and v2 over-grades "measured" much less (3 of 35 measured cards on the sample, against 13 of 44 in v1). Tier is unchanged at 67.5%. realtime_infra moved the other way from what the rubric intended: v2 flags 29.8% of all cards against v1's 25.8%. It drops feed filters and search tools but now flags almost every game and more trading bots. It agrees slightly more with the hand labels (91.7%) because it misses no realtime card on the sample, but it over-flags 10 of 36. The framing shifts are not reported: framing is withdrawn.

## base
not_a_jev_build is mostly posts that v1 put in other_or_meta (146 of the 221 that v1 labelled; the other 13 arrived after v1): local clones, distillations, "Jev-compatible" APIs over other models, and builds on Laya, Gemini or Qwen. 29 were games in v1, such as an on-device Laya port playing Snake. The most viewed is a distilled 4B "Jev-style" local model (426,247 views), followed by a local Jev clone (206,466 views). Duplicates are few under the specified rule (7), and the looser chips-only rule would add at most 24 more.

## families
Four families hold 64% of posts: other_or_meta (21.1%), games (18.9%), classification, routing and triage (15.5%) and agent harness (8.3%). Views and likes do not follow posts, and the two measures agree. Browser and computer use has 3.9% of posts, 12.8% of views and 12.1% of likes. Live chat, compaction and collaboration draw 2.2 to 3.5 times their post share of views or likes, and trading draws 1.6 to 1.8 times. Games draw about two thirds of their share (0.66 of views, 0.68 of likes) and evals about half (0.54 and 0.51). Voice is the one family where likes (0.60) run well ahead of views (0.38). Family is the second least stable label on the audit's re-read: it agrees with the model on 71% of the audited cards, on 75% in its two largest random samples (no comparison named, demos with no numbers) and on 58% of the voice, live chat and collaboration cards.



## evidence
64.5% of posts show a build with no numbers and 33.8% report a number from the author's own demo or experiment. Measured demos draw 51.4% of views and 47.7% of likes. 15 posts (0.3%) meet v2's production test; on the audit's re-read of all 15, 8 hold (0.14% of posts). Evidence is the most stable field: it agrees with the audit on 89% of the audited cards.

## evidence_by_family
Data and telemetry (65% measured), trading (49%) and classification (43%) are the most measured families. Collaboration (20%), other_or_meta (21%) and live chat (24%) are the least.



## baseline
On the model's labels 81.4% of posts compare Jev with nothing; the audit corrects that to 78.2% (75.7 to 79.8%). Of the 1,062 posts that make a comparison, 690 (65%) compare with a frontier LLM, the weakest baseline for a narrow decision. Rules or regex (94), classic ML (77) and vendor APIs (30) together are 201 posts (3.5%), and the audit's estimate is the same share, 3.5% (2.8 to 5.1%).

## baseline_by_family
Browser (21%), agent harness (20%), classification (19%) and evals (18%) compare with a frontier LLM most often. Search (5%) and moderation (4%) name rules most often. Classic ML and vendor APIs are 4% or less in every family.

## concentration
Views are highly concentrated (Gini 0.94) and so are likes (0.93). 49 cards hold half of all views, the median card has 134 views and 2 likes, and 44.8% of cards have under 100 views. The top-1% share is robust: it stays between 47.5% and 53.4% whether we measure by likes, drop the top card, drop the suspect cards, or use all 5,950 raw cards, and the audit's recount gives the same 53.4%.

## by_day
Post age drives the counts. The median post from 16 September has 793 views and from 22 September 93. Posts from 16 to 19 September are half of all posts and hold 79% of views and 77% of likes. Late-week families look less popular than they are. Trading is the clearest case: its daily count peaked on 21 and 22 September (55 and 44 posts, against 27 to 38 a day from 17 to 20 September).

## top20
9 of the 20 most-viewed cards are measured demos, 10 are demos with no numbers and 1 is commentary; none is measured production. Browser and computer use (5), classification (3) and agent harness (3) are the most common families. Two cards moved into use-case families in v2: the Laya-vs-Jev Snake benchmark (games) and a probabilistic programming language built on Jev (agent harness).

## realtime
Games are 59% of the flagged realtime cards. Outside games, trading (172) and browser agents (126) are the largest groups. Only 2 realtime cards are measured production, and neither holds on the audit's re-read: one is a live news feed with no number in the text, the other a test of 288 labelled calls. On the first review's re-read of v1, 3 of v1's 16 realtime production cards held up; 9 of the 16 were trading bots that trade the author's own money.

## realtime_families
On the model's labels the three realtime families are 2.7% of posts (v1: 2.9%). The audit's census and negative sample put them at 3.8% (2.7 to 6.3%), so the labels undercount them a little, and none is in production or claims to be. Japanese posts are 36% to 38% of these families against 19.7% overall. They draw more attention than their size (6.2% of views).


## production
Three readings set the range. The audit's re-read keeps 8 of the 15 strict v2 cards (0.14% of posts), and its samples allow at most 2.0%. The first review's re-read of v1's 95 production cards kept 51 (0.9% of v1's 5,782 cards), a looser reading that the audit did not repeat, and the first pass's 1.6% is the ceiling of the looser labels. The 8 that hold are a task router, a deploy approval gate, a news pipeline, a search reranker, an episode recommender, a fleet-wide decision layer and two public Q&A sites. None is a realtime build.

## posts_per_day
Posting peaked on 19 September (1,047 posts in the base) and fell to 644 on 22 September. 16 September has only 93 posts: the feed's first post is at 00:28 UTC, about 6 hours after launch, and posting ramped up from 17 September. 23 September is a partial day (last post 18:44 UTC).

## language
English is 68.6% of posts, Japanese 19.7% and Chinese 6.5%. Chinese posts draw 12.3% of views but 8.2% of likes, and English posts draw 74.7% of likes from 68.6% of posts. Japanese is highest in live chat and voice (38% each) and collaboration (36%). Chinese is highest in trading (14%) and browser (10%).

## chips
The two multiples are the only chip medians we keep, and they agree with OpenChamber's survey within a factor of about 1.2. The "1×" chips are an extractor artefact (52 of the 299 speed chips and 8 of the 126 cost chips). Dropping them moves the medians to 34× and 8.5×. The audit recounted both medians, and they hold.

## agreement
Agreement with Sonnet barely moves from v1 (77.2%, on the 5,782 cards v1 labelled) to v2 (76.8%), although v2 changed 13.6% of family labels. The pattern is the same as in v1. Games (F1 0.90), trading (0.91) and compaction (0.88) match best, and live chat (0.47), collaboration (0.49) and data and telemetry (0.59) worst. Jev folds agent-harness and data cards into classification or other_or_meta. The largest disagreement is evals cards that Jev calls other_or_meta (88). Against the hand labels, Jev (70.8%) and Sonnet v2 (73.3%) are within each other's 95% intervals.

## calibration
Agreement rises steadily with Jev's stated probability, from 37.8% in the lowest decile to 98.8% in the highest, and the curve is almost the same against v1 and v2. The two highest deciles (probability 0.99 or more) agree 97.3% and 98.8% of the time, but 38% of the cards at 0.99 or more are games or trading, the easiest families. Against the hand labels, Jev is right on 27 of 28 at 0.99 or more and 17 of 38 below 0.7; on the audit's 464 cards at 0.99 or more, it matches the audit's family on 455 (98.1%). This measures consistency with a reader, not accuracy against a human.

## hand_check
v2 improves on v1 in every field except tier, which is unchanged. The biggest gains are evidence (+10.0 points) and baseline (+7.5). v2 is also not deterministic. Between pilot 2 and the full run, on the same 120 cards and the same rubric, family matched on 102 and tier on 98. So a gap of about 5 points between two runs is noise. The hand labels are from one careful AI reader. The audit (1,681 cards, a different AI reader) is the larger check. In its two largest random samples it agrees on family about as often as these 120 cards (75%); on the voice, live chat and collaboration cards (58%) and the 91 candidate builds (59%) it agrees much less. The 120 human labels in [human-labels-120.csv](human-labels-120.csv) are the check that is still missing.

## ladder
Two thirds of posts (66.0%) show no measurement on the model's labels, and they hold 48.4% of views; measured demos are a third of posts (33.8%) and hold half the views (51.4%). The audit splits the unmeasured posts that are not meta into demos with no numbers (about 35%) and claims with no number (about 16%). The top rung is nearly empty: 8 posts.
